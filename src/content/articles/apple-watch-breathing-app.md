## The short answer

Apple's built-in Mindfulness app runs Breathe sessions of one to five minutes at a breath rate you can adjust<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>. It will not take a named pattern like 4-7-8. Five third-party Apple Watch apps will, and a plain [breathing pacer](/breathing-pacer) covers the rest.

The word "app" is doing a lot of work in that search. Some people want a guided library; some want an editor where they can type 4-7-8; some already know their pattern and want a rhythm on the wrist with nothing else attached. Those are three different products, and the last one is a timer rather than a breathing app. This page sorts them out, with every competitor fact read on that app's own App Store listing.

## What the built-in Breathe app does well — and where it stops

The Mindfulness app is the default answer, and for a lot of people it is the right one. It is already on the Watch, it costs nothing, and it cues you by feel. Apple's guide describes choosing a session "between 1 and 5 minutes," tapping Breath Rate "to change the number of breaths per minute," and tapping Haptics to "choose None, Minimal, or Prominent"<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>.

Three limits show up once you have a specific pattern in mind.

**The ceiling is five minutes.** For a ten- or twenty-minute sit you are restarting sessions<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>.

**It paces a breath rate, not a pattern.** You set how many breaths per minute; you do not set four seconds in, seven held, eight out.

**It ends with a screen.** Your heart rate "appears in the Summary screen" when a session completes<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> — useful data, and also an interruption.

None of this makes it a bad app. It makes it a general-purpose one, and structured breathwork is specific: a systematic review defines the field as bringing the breath below roughly ten breaths per minute<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup>. If your pattern has holds in it, you need something that knows about holds.

## What to look for in a third-party breathing app

- **Custom patterns.** Can you enter inhale, hold, exhale, hold — or only pick from a menu?
- **Phase-specific cues.** Does the Watch distinguish inhale from exhale, or is every cue the same?
- **Standalone on the Watch.** Does it run on its own, or need the phone in the room?
- **Haptics rather than a screen.** A pacer that needs your eyes is fighting the exercise.
- **Offline, no account, clear price.** Check the listing — these change.

## Where *AtLeast* fits — and where it does not

*AtLeast* solves a neighbouring problem; it would be dishonest to file it under "breathing app."

It is a passive haptic timer for Apple Watch. You set a minimum duration between one and sixty minutes and a tap interval; gentle taps mark time on your wrist, and **when the taps stop you have reached your minimum**. No alarm, no sound, no summary screen — "set a floor, not a ceiling."

For breathwork it behaves as a metronome. Box breathing's four equal phases map to a four-second interval; coherent breathing at 5-5 maps to five seconds. For 4-7-8 you can set a one-second interval and count taps instead of counting in your head.

**What it does not do:**

- **No per-phase cues.** One tap interval per session; every tap feels the same. For uneven patterns, Box Breathe or Paced Breathing will serve you better.
- **No guided sessions, library, or pattern editor.** Duration and interval are the whole interface.

What it gives you: it runs **entirely on the Watch, offline**, taps **continue with the watch face off**, there is **no account**, and analytics are cookieless. It needs Apple Watch Series 6 or later on watchOS 26.0 or later, and it is a free TestFlight beta today. Wrist tap intensity is a watchOS setting — Default or Prominent<sup><a href="#ref-8">[8]</a></sup>.

## Which one to pick

- **Simplest thing for five minutes or less.** Built-in Mindfulness.
- **Pattern editor and phase-specific taps, paid once.** Box Breathe<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>.
- **Custom rhythm with progress tracking, subscription OK.** Paced Breathing or Unwind.
- **You already know your pattern and want a beat with nothing attached.** *AtLeast*.
- **Guided meditation as well as breathing.** See our [comparison page](/compare).

Slow breathing is generally considered safe for healthy people, but none of these apps is a medical device. Stop if you feel dizzy or light-headed, and never practise breath holds in or near water.
