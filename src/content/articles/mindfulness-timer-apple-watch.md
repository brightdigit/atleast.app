## What people mean by a mindfulness timer on Apple Watch

Search for a mindfulness timer on Apple Watch and you usually land on one of two things: Apple's built-in Mindfulness app, or a timer for a longer silent sit. They are both useful. They are not the same job.

Apple's Mindfulness app is already on your wrist. It is good at what it was designed for — a few minutes to focus, center, and breathe. When the practice you want is a ten-, twenty-, or thirty-minute sit with your eyes closed, you need something else: a timer that marks time without asking you to watch a screen or wait for an alarm. Our fuller guide to an [Apple Watch meditation timer](/apple-watch-meditation-timer) covers session length and intervals across the cluster; this article is the bridge from the built-in app to a dedicated practice timer.

*AtLeast* is that timer: gentle taps during the sit, silence when you reach your minimum, offline on the Watch, no account.

## What Apple's Mindfulness app actually does

Apple's user guide is clear about the product. The Mindfulness app "encourages you to set aside a few minutes a day to focus, center, and connect as you breathe."<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> Inside it you get Reflect, Breathe, and State of Mind.

**Reflect.** Tap Reflect, read the theme, focus your attention, then tap Begin.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> It is a short, themed attention exercise — useful as a reset, not a container for an open sit.

**Breathe.** Tap Breathe, inhale as the animation grows, exhale as it shrinks.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> You can set breath rate and choose haptics of None, Minimal, or Prominent.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> The pacing lives on the screen. That is the opposite of eyes-closed practice.

**Duration.** For both Reflect and Breathe, Apple states you can choose a time between 1 and 5 minutes.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> That ceiling is intentional. The app is a short break, not a longer sit.

**State of Mind.** Separately, you can log a momentary emotion or daily mood from the same app, with optional descriptors for what is having the biggest impact on you.<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup> That is wellbeing logging, not timing.

**Health summary.** After a Reflect or Breathe session, heart rate appears on the Summary screen and can be reviewed later in Health.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> Logging to Health is something a third-party timer will not match — and for a short guided break, that is a real advantage.

If a one-to-five-minute Reflect or Breathe session is what you want, use the built-in app. There is no reason to replace it.

## Where the built-in app falls short for silent sits

The gap shows up the moment the practice outgrows five minutes or needs the eyes closed the whole way.

**The ceiling is five minutes.** Harvard Health suggests beginners start with five minutes and work toward 10 to 20 — "the range most clinical programs recommend."<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup> Mindful's beginner guide suggests setting a short limit of about 5–10 minutes.<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> Apple's Mindfulness range stops where those recommendations start to grow.

**Breathe asks you to look.** The instruction is to follow a growing and shrinking animation.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> A silent sit is the opposite: nothing to watch, nothing to unlock, nothing lighting a dark room.

**It completes as a session, not a floor.** Reflect and Breathe run their length and finish. There is no framing in which continuing past the end is expected, and nothing during the session that says *time is passing, keep going* without also ending the break.

**The built-in Timer is the other common workaround — and a different problem.** The Timers app will happily count twenty minutes, but when a timer goes off you get an alert; Apple's guide describes tapping to start another of the same duration afterward.<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup> Silent Mode mutes alerts and sound effects while you can still receive haptic notifications,<sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup> so the ending can be a buzz instead of a chime. That still leaves you with one signal at the end, nothing during the sit, and a device-wide mute you have to remember to turn on and off. For shared spaces and early mornings, see our notes on a [silent meditation timer](/silent-meditation-timer) and a [meditation timer without sound](/meditation-timer-without-sound).

None of this makes Mindfulness deficient. It was built for a short guided break. The gap between that break and a real practice timer is exactly what a dedicated app is for. A side-by-side with other options lives on our [comparison page](/compare).

## What a dedicated mindfulness timer adds

*AtLeast* is a passive haptic timer for Apple Watch. You set a minimum duration (1–60 minutes) and a tap interval, press Start on the Watch, and put your attention elsewhere.

**Mid-session haptic nudges.** Gentle taps arrive at the interval you chose — sparse markers every few minutes, or a steadier rhythm every 30 or 60 seconds. They carry information rather than instruction: *you are still here*. Apple's Mindfulness haptics pace a short Breathe session;<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> here the taps run through a longer sit you chose yourself.

**Silence as completion.** When the taps stop, you have reached your minimum. No alarm, no closing buzz, no screen. The absence of the rhythm is the signal. That leaves the closing passage intact — Mindful's "close with kindness," noticing the room and how the body feels,<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup> and Harvard's brief pause afterward to notice whether tension has eased.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

**A floor, not a ceiling.** If the sit is going well when the taps stop, nothing is asking you to get up. Set *at least* ten minutes and keep sitting if you want to.

**Eyes-free and offline.** The session continues with the watch face off. It runs entirely on the Watch with no phone nearby — a basement studio, airplane mode, a retreat with no signal. An optional iPhone companion can pre-load or pick a timer on a larger screen and mirror the live session; the taps always happen on the wrist.

**No account.** Nothing to sign in to, no streak, no library of guided content. Session and haptic logic run on-device. Analytics are cookieless (website via Plausible; iPhone and Watch via AviaryInsights) and never used for advertising or cross-app tracking.

It requires Apple Watch Series 6 or later running watchOS 26.0 or later, and it is currently a free [TestFlight](https://testflight.apple.com/join/WUR3Wf47) beta.

## A practical way to use both

Many people will keep both. Use Mindfulness when you want Apple's short Reflect or Breathe break and the Health summary that comes with it.<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> Switch to a practice timer when the sit needs to be longer, silent, and eyes-free.

| Need | Built-in Mindfulness | *AtLeast* |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 minute guided break | Yes — Reflect or Breathe<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> | Possible, but not what it is for |
| Eyes-closed silent sit | No — Breathe is screen-paced<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> | Yes — taps only, face can sleep |
| 10–20+ minutes | No — duration maxes at 5<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> | Yes — minimum 1–60 minutes |
| Mid-session orientation | Session runs, then ends | Taps at your interval |
| Ending without an alert | Session completes on its own terms | Taps stop; silence is the signal |
| Health heart-rate summary | Yes, after Reflect/Breathe<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup> | No — it is a timer, not a Health workout |

Suggested starting points: a 10-minute minimum with a 5-minute interval for a quiet morning sit; 20 minutes with a 5-minute interval for a standard silent practice; 10 minutes with a 30-second interval when the mind will not settle and a steady rhythm helps. Harvard's guidance still holds — consistency matters more than duration.<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>

Meditation is generally considered low-risk; the NCCIH notes these practices "usually are considered to have few risks," while also reporting that in one review about 8 percent of participants described negative effects, most commonly anxiety or depression.<sup><a href="#ref-7">[7]</a></sup> It is not a substitute for medical or psychological care, and *AtLeast* is a timer, not a medical device.

Use Mindfulness for the short break it was built for. When the practice needs a longer silent container, set a floor on the wrist and let the quiet tell you when you have earned it.
