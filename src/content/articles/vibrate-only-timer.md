A vibrate-only timer never makes sound. It cues you by touch alone — a tap on the wrist, not a chime — so there is nothing to mute, turn down, or accidentally leave on. On Apple Watch that usually means a [timer that vibrates](/timer-that-vibrates) by design, not a countdown you quiet after the fact.

The phrase sounds like a setting. It is really a design choice: vibrate as a fallback when sound is off, versus silent from the first tap to the last absence of one.

## Vibrate-as-fallback vs silent-by-design

**Vibrate-as-fallback** is what most people already have. Put the phone on vibrate, or turn on Apple Watch Silent Mode, and the same timer still runs — it just swaps speakers for a motor. Apple’s own guidance is clear: Silent Mode mutes alerts, notifications, and sound effects, and you still receive haptic notifications<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>. In Sounds & Haptics you can set wrist-tap intensity to Default, Prominent, or Off<sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>.

That works. It also has two limits that matter for practice.

First, the built-in Timer still speaks once: a buzz when the countdown expires. Nothing marks time while you are in the session. You either glance and break the state, or wait for a stop signal that arrives when attention is deepest.

Second, mute is a switch, not a guarantee. Apple notes that while the Watch is charging, alarms and timers can still sound even in Silent Mode<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>. A fallback path that can regain a voice is not the same as having no voice.

**Silent-by-design** starts from the opposite premise. The timer has no sound channel. Every cue is touch. Mid-session taps repeat at an interval you choose; when they stop, you have reached your minimum. There is no end chime to soften, no volume to mis-set, and nothing for a charging cable to re-enable.

That mid-session rhythm is the practical difference. Continuously informing tactile displays — signals that keep telling you state rather than only alarming — have been shown to support attention under high demand better than a tactile alarm alone<sup><a href="#ref-6">[6]</a></sup>. A vibrate-only practice timer uses the same idea at a gentler scale: taps mean “time is still passing,” not “stop now.”

## Where vibrate-only matters most

**Shared spaces.** An office focus block, a quiet classroom, a studio, a bedroom with someone asleep — a beep is a broadcast. A wrist tap reaches only you. Silent Mode and Theater Mode already lean on this: Theater Mode keeps the display dark and still delivers haptics<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup>. A vibrate-only timer goes further by never owning a sound to leak into the room.

**Meditation.** Many sits ask for a quiet place and sustained attention on breath, a mantra, or present-moment awareness<sup><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup>. A closing bell cuts across the settled minutes. A timer that ends by going quiet leaves the transition yours: open your eyes when you are ready, not when a speaker decides.

**Breathwork.** Patterns such as box breathing are simple counting — four in, four hold, four out, four hold — and they work anywhere you can breathe without straining<sup><a href="#ref-5">[5]</a></sup>. Eyes are often closed; counting is easier against a steady wrist tap than against a screen. *AtLeast* uses one interval per session (it does not cue inhale versus exhale as separate events). For even 4-4-4-4, a four-second interval can mark each phase change; for uneven patterns, use a short steady interval you count against.

In each case the requirement is the same: time without announcing time to anyone else, and without pulling your eyes open to check.

## How AtLeast stays vibrate-only

*AtLeast* is a passive haptic timer for Apple Watch built around that silent-by-design path.

You set a **minimum** duration (1–60 minutes) and a **tap interval**. Gentle taps mark the session on your wrist. When the taps stop, you have earned the floor. There is no alarm and no sound at any point — silence is the completion signal, and you can keep going if you want.

On the Watch:

- Haptics continue with the **watch face off**, so you never need to raise your wrist.
- It runs **entirely on Apple Watch**, works **offline**, and needs no phone nearby.
- An optional iPhone companion can pre-load or pick a timer and mirror the live session; the taps still happen on the wrist.
- Requires **Apple Watch Series 6 or later** on **watchOS 26.0 or later**.
- Free TestFlight beta today — no App Store listing yet, no accounts to create.

Privacy stays light on purpose. Session and haptic logic run on-device. Analytics are cookieless page and session signals only — never for advertising or following you across other apps. A timer that only taps should not need to know who you are.

## When Silent Mode is enough

If you only need one silent “time’s up” buzz — pasta, laundry, a short stretch — the built-in Timer plus Silent Mode is already on your wrist and free<sup><a href="#ref-1">[1]</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-2">[2]</a></sup>. Use it.

Reach for vibrate-only when the session itself is what you are protecting: meditation, breathwork, a plunge, a shared room, or any practice where a mid-session pulse helps more than a final buzz, and where “never makes sound” has to mean never — not “muted until something flips the switch back.”
